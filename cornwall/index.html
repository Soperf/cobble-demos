The Keepers & The House

Restored by a fisherman and a ceramicist.

“Built for St Ives’ chief harbour pilot in 1812, this granite house has held two centuries of Atlantic weather. We wanted it to feel like coming home to a quiet coastal sanctuary.”

We are Nathan Rosewarne and Freya Miller. Nathan spent twenty seasons fishing these coastal waters aboard local pilchard luggers, and Freya throws salt-glazed stoneware in her studio just down Back Road West.

When we restored the house, we removed layers of modern wallpaper to uncover original lime-washed granite, maritime pitch-pine beams, and high sash windows that drink in the luminous Cornish light.

Breakfast is served around our long scrubbed-pine refectory table: freshly landed St Ives pilchards grilled with wild sea fennel, artisan sourdough from Fore Street bakery, Cornish clotted cream butter, and seasonal preserves served in Freya’s handmade bowls.

The Setting & Arrival

On the Harbour Wall

A quiet lane from everything, yet insulated from the crowds

By Scenic Rail

Arrive via the iconic single-track St Ives Bay branch line from St Erth. Step off the train and take the gentle 6-minute stroll past the harbour beach straight to our door on The Wharf.

By Car & Parking

Driving into medieval St Ives is notoriously challenging, but we eliminate the hassle: every direct booking includes a complimentary reserved permit for the secure harbour car park.

On Foot & Surf

The South West Coast Path runs past our front step. Porthmeor surf beach is 4 minutes on foot, Tate St Ives is 3 minutes away, and coastal passenger boats moor right opposite.
